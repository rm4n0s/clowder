Step 20.2: the plans and the timings of the unread counts (SPEC §7.17, D55)

What this measures: the query that gives the unread posts and the unread
mentions of every room of one user (store.RoomUnreadCounts, the sidebar and
GET /rooms), its one-room form (store.RoomUnreadOf, the stream that patches one
entry of the sidebar and the WebSocket event room.unread) and the highest ID of a
room (store.RoomMaxPostID, used when a member joins, opens the feed or marks it).

Data: `clowder demo -size large` (SPEC §10): 200 users, 100 rooms (14 private),
100,000 posts and comments (70,250 top-level), 124,556 audit events. The user is
the one with the most memberships, made a member of all 100 rooms; 300 unread
mention notifications are added for the user. The marks are put where each case
says. Medians of 30 runs for all rooms and 100 runs for one room.

Machine: the development machine of docs/PERFORMANCE.md (32 CPUs, local disk),
SQLite through the pure-Go driver, PostgreSQL 18.6 in a container on the same
machine (fsync off).

How to repeat: make a large demo (`go run ./cmd/clowder demo -data DIR -size
large`, with `-database-url postgres://...` for PostgreSQL) and run
  CLOWDER_BENCH_DB=DIR/clowder.db go test ./internal/store -run LargeDemoUnreadPlans -v
  CLOWDER_BENCH_DATABASE_URL=postgres://... go test ./internal/store -run LargeDemoUnreadPlans -v
The test fails when the median for 100 rooms with nothing seen is over 20 ms.

Summary
-------
No new index is needed. The posts of a room that are new are found by the index
of the feed: idx_posts_feed (room_id, parent_id, id DESC) on SQLite and the
partial index idx_posts_feed_roots (room_id, id DESC) WHERE parent_id IS NULL on
PostgreSQL (migration 0002); the mentions by idx_notifications_user_read
(user_id, read_at, id); the marks by the primary key of room_reads (user_id,
room_id).

The count stops at 100 per room (store.UnreadPostsCap; the pages show "99+"
above 99). Before the cap, a user who had not read any of the 100 rooms (66,704
unread posts) took 29 to 31 ms on PostgreSQL (target: under 20 ms) and 59 ms on
SQLite, because the query counted every unread post of every room. With the cap
the worst case is 100 rooms of 100 rows:

                                   PostgreSQL    SQLite
  nothing seen, capped (worst)      11.6 ms       9.0 ms   <- the target case: under 20 ms
  nothing seen, no cap (exact)      30.7 ms      59.3 ms
  half seen                          5.8 ms       8.6 ms
  everything seen                    0.3 ms       0.4 ms
  one room, busiest, nothing seen    0.5 ms       0.09 ms
  highest ID of the busiest room     0.04 ms      0.9 ms

A new post to a room of N members makes N one-room counts (one per open stream
of a member): 0.1 to 0.5 ms each when the member has unread posts, 0.05 ms when
the member has read the room.
